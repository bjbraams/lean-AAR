**Statements and definitions to repair before attempting proofs**

1. **The current `confluentU` definition does not implement its stated integer-parameter continuation.** In [Confluent/Basic.lean:381](AAR/Confluent/Basic.lean#L381), it is a raw Gamma-prefactor connection formula. Mathlib's Gamma function is zero at its poles. Consequently, at `b = 1` both prefactors vanish, and **`confluentU a 1 z = 0` for every `a,z`**. I verified this in Lean without admitted dependencies. This contradicts `confluentU_of_zero_param` in [Relations.lean:60](AAR/Confluent/Relations.lean#L60), which asserts the value one at `a = 0`. Many integer-parameter identities and large-argument normalizations are blocked by the same issue. The docstring says the integer values are limits, but the definition does not take those limits. A genuine continuation definition, with a theorem establishing the relevant limit, is needed if these all-parameter statements are to be retained.

   The analogous raw definition of `whittakerW` in [Whittaker.lean:37](AAR/Confluent/Whittaker.lean#L37) degenerates at `μ = 0`. Its generic-parameter docstring is more candid, but unconditional downstream formulas still require review. In contrast, the Bessel `Y` and `K` definitions already use a limiting branch for integer order; they should not be grouped with this exact defect. Their limit-existence API remains a separate proof task.

2. **`polygamma_multiplication` includes an invalid order-zero case.** [Gamma/Polygamma.lean:176](AAR/Gamma/Polygamma.lean#L176) accepts `n = 0`. At that order, the digamma multiplication formula has an additional `log m` term. For example, `m = 2`, `z = 1` satisfies the stated pole exclusions but the displayed right side is short by `log 2`. Require `0 < n`, or provide a separate order-zero formula. The referenced [DLMF 5.15.7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.15#E7) uses positive derivative order. This is a mathematical counterexample, not one of the Lean counterexample probes.

3. **`qGamma_mono_right` has too broad a range for `x`.** [Gamma/QGamma.lean:102](AAR/Gamma/QGamma.lean#L102) asserts strict decrease in `q` for every `x > 1`. At `x = 2`, the already proved `qGamma_two` makes both values one. I checked the resulting negation of the strict inequality in Lean. Restrict the theorem to `1 < x < 2`, matching [DLMF 5.18.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.18#E9); the direction differs beyond two.

4. **`Gamma_ratio_norm_le` is false even for positive real Gamma arguments.** In [Gamma/Inequalities.lean:80](AAR/Gamma/Inequalities.lean#L80), take `x = 2`, `a = -1`, `b = 0`. Both assumptions hold, but the conclusion becomes `1 ≤ 1/2`. Lean verified the numerical contradiction without admitted dependencies. Merely excluding Gamma poles will not repair this statement; its parameter restrictions or bound must change.

5. **`digamma_half_diff` confuses an ordered conditional series with Lean's unordered sum.** At `z = 1`, the series in [Gamma/SeriesExpansions.lean:74](AAR/Gamma/SeriesExpansions.lean#L74) is alternating harmonic. It is not summable in Mathlib's `HasSum` sense over `ℂ`, so its `tsum` is zero, whereas the left side is `2 log 2`. Express convergence of ordered partial sums, or pair adjacent terms into the absolutely summable series `1/(2k+z) - 1/(2k+1+z)`. The local `alternating_digamma` in [Hypergeometric/Elementary.lean:219](AAR/Hypergeometric/Elementary.lean#L219) already uses ordered partial sums. The classical formula is [DLMF 5.7.7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.7#E7). This mathematical diagnosis was not separately formalized in the review probes.

6. **`Gamma_ratio_expansion` uses incomplete coefficients as if they gave arbitrary-order asymptotics.** [Gamma/Asymptotics.lean:92](AAR/Gamma/Asymptotics.lean#L92) defines `stirlingRatioG` with only its first two coefficients nonzero, while the theorem at line 98 quantifies over every truncation order. Take `a = 3`, `b = 0`, `n = 3`. The exact ratio is `t³ + 3t² + 2t`, but the declared approximation is `t³ + 3t²`; the claim would make `2t = O(1)`. Supply the actual coefficient sequence or restrict the expansion to justified orders. This is an algebraic mathematical counterexample, not a Lean-checked asymptotic counterexample.

7. **Existing warnings about limits and monodromy remain actionable.** The comments around `tendsto_confluentM_div_Gamma_at_neg_nat` in [BasicProperties.lean:101](AAR/Confluent/BasicProperties.lean#L101) identify the need to distinguish a punctured parameter limit from the assigned value at a pole. The comments around `whittakerM_neg_argument` and `whittakerM_continuation` in [Whittaker.lean:227](AAR/Confluent/Whittaker.lean#L227) and [238](AAR/Confluent/Whittaker.lean#L238) identify branch/continuation issues. Multiplying an argument by `exp (2πi)` returns the same complex number and does not itself encode analytic continuation around a singularity. These are pre-existing identified issues, not newly discovered completed-theorem failures.

**Easy admissions that would give misleading progress**

`confluentU_poincare_finite` and `confluentU_poincare_remainder`, [Confluent/Asymptotics.lean:61](AAR/Confluent/Asymptotics.lean#L61) and [69](AAR/Confluent/Asymptotics.lean#L69), merely assert existence of an additive error term. Choosing the error as the difference proves either statement for any function whatsoever. Neither gives a bound on that error. They are easy to discharge, but should first be strengthened if they are intended to formalize a useful remainder result.

Similarly, `regularizedHGFun_poincare_sector` at [line 43](AAR/Confluent/Asymptotics.lean#L43) is an `IsBigO` statement at `𝓝 z`, not along large arguments in a sector. Its order parameter `n` does not occur in the conclusion. It expresses local boundedness after subtracting a constant, far less than a Poincaré expansion.

Some completed results also have narrower content than their names suggest. `jacobiP_matrix_coefficient` in [SphericalHarmonics/Theorems.lean:96](AAR/SphericalHarmonics/Theorems.lean#L96) proves a reflexive identity; the comment acknowledges the missing representation-theoretic formulation. `dyson_constant_term` in [Selberg/Applications.lean:88](AAR/Selberg/Applications.lean#L88) identifies a factorial quotient with a multinomial coefficient, without constructing or extracting the claimed Laurent-polynomial constant term. Their proofs are valid, but they do not establish the full named results.

**Generality, names, namespaces, and documentation**

| Review question | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Are statements in the most general appropriate setting? | Not uniformly. Reciprocal Gamma's Taylor theorem can be global; several other statements instead need stronger hypotheses or corrected definitions. The general measure-based orthogonal-polynomial development is a good reusable foundation. Complex parameters are appropriate for much of the analytic theory; indiscriminate abstraction beyond `ℂ` would not improve branch-sensitive results. |
| Are capitalization and underscores conventional? | Largely, with exceptions and room to improve theorem phrasing. Definitions generally use lower camel case; predicates such as `IsBaileyPair` appropriately use upper camel case. Theorem names generally use underscores while retaining the spelling of referenced functions. |
| Are theorem names descriptive of their statements? | Uneven. Many are good; several follow textbook topic labels rather than the actual conclusion, and the placeholder cases above are materially misleading. |
| Does every declaration have a formal docstring? | **No.** 609 have one; **1,062 do not**: 826 theorems, 186 lemmas, and 50 definitions. Seventeen missing docstrings are on private declarations. A DLMF attribute or ordinary comment does not constitute a formal docstring. |
| Do structure fields have documentation? | No explicit project structures/classes or their fields were found, so this part is not applicable. Predicate definitions are included in the declaration audit. |
| Are namespaces appropriate? | Mixed. Natural namespaces such as `Complex`, `Real`, and `MeasureTheory` coexist with `AAR.*` and root-level special-function declarations. This is workable locally but should be made more consistent as APIs are prepared for Mathlib. |
| Is every Lean source file a module? | **Yes.** All 96 have an explicit `module` declaration. The implementation modules are reachable from `AAR.lean`; `Main.lean` is the separate executable root. |
| Does every file have a module docstring? | **Yes.** All 96 contain a formal module header. Quality and completeness vary: presence does not imply an adequate account of definitions, notation, main results, or proof status. |

Mathlib's convention is lower camel case for ordinary data-valued definitions, upper camel case for propositions/types, and underscore-separated theorem names, with embedded identifiers retaining the prescribed spelling. The convention also favors names that describe the conclusion. See the [Mathlib naming guide](https://leanprover-community.github.io/contribute/naming.html).

Concrete naming improvements to consider during later edits:

- `inv_Gamma_hasSum` would be easier to discover as `hasSum_invGammaCoeff` or `hasSum_inv_Gamma`, consistently with its `HasSum` conclusion.
- `laguerre_norm` evaluates a weighted integral of a square rather than a Lean `norm` expression. A name such as `integral_laguerre_sq` would describe the formal statement more directly.
- `qGamma_mono_right` proves a strict inequality in the base parameter, with direction and range currently incorrect. After repair, a name describing the strict antitonicity and its parameter would communicate more than `mono_right`.
- The capitalized data-valued definition `GammaInc` in [Confluent/Relations.lean:73](AAR/Confluent/Relations.lean#L73) deserves an explicit naming decision, ideally coordinated with `lowerIncompleteGamma` and any intended Mathlib incomplete-Gamma API. Established `Complex.Gamma` spelling alone is not a reason to indiscriminately rename every `Gamma` occurrence in theorem names.

These are targeted API recommendations, not a proposal for a broad mechanical rename. Generic lemmas belong with the underlying object where practicable; for example, the measure/OP statements should be discoverable from their predicate or mathematical namespace. A book-specific namespace is reasonable for staging, but the final Mathlib API should not require knowing the chapter organization. Some root-level functions and `AAR.*` theorem families currently split that discoverability.

Documentation should be repaired alongside the next proofs. For example, `squaredNorm`, `christoffelDarbouxKernel`, `moment`, and `hankelDeterminant` at the beginning of [GeneralIdentities.lean](AAR/OrthogonalPolynomials/GeneralIdentities.lean) lack formal docstrings despite being public definitions. Explain the weighting, indexing and totalized integral behavior, not just a source formula number. For analytic functions, document the actual pole values and branch conventions; the `confluentU` mismatch is a concrete example of why this matters.

Module headers should name the main definitions and proved entry points, describe introduced notation where there is any, and distinguish established results from admitted coverage. Many chapter-style headers give only a topic and DLMF range. The one-line umbrella headers and `README.md`'s unfinished organization section do not give a useful status overview. Some headers refer to `DLMFCoverage.md`, which is absent from the project root; those references need updating. The historical [argument-continuation design note](DESIGN_2F1_ARGUMENT_CONTINUATION.md) also needs clearer separation of its implemented status from its older discussion of missing work.

**Appendix: the three checked proof candidates**

The following was checked against the current project with `lake env lean --stdin`. The temporary theorem names avoid collisions with the existing admitted declarations. The reciprocal-Gamma statement deliberately omits the unnecessary disk hypothesis. In a later implementation, the proof bodies can replace the corresponding admissions, with appropriate imports and formal docstrings.

```lean
import AAR
import Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.TaylorSeries

open Complex Polynomial MeasureTheory
open scoped Real

namespace AARReview

theorem inv_gamma_series (z : ℂ) :
    HasSum (fun k : ℕ => AAR.Gamma.invGammaCoeff k * z ^ k) (Complex.Gamma z)⁻¹ := by
  simpa [AAR.Gamma.invGammaCoeff, div_eq_mul_inv, smul_eq_mul,
    mul_comm, mul_left_comm, mul_assoc] using
    Complex.hasSum_taylorSeries_of_entire Complex.differentiable_one_div_Gamma 0 z

theorem hermite_integral (n : ℕ) (x : ℝ) :
    ∫ y : ℝ in (0 : ℝ)..x, (aeval y (hermitePhys n) : ℝ) =
      (aeval x (hermitePhys (n + 1)) - aeval (0 : ℝ) (hermitePhys (n + 1))) /
        (2 * (n + 1)) := by
  have hd (t : ℝ) : HasDerivAt (fun y : ℝ => aeval y (hermitePhys (n + 1)))
      (2 * (n + 1) * aeval t (hermitePhys n)) t := by
    have h := (hermitePhys (n + 1)).hasDerivAt_aeval t
    rw [hermitePhys_derivative] at h
    simpa only [map_mul, aeval_C, map_ofNat, map_add, map_natCast, map_one] using h
  have hi : IntervalIntegrable (fun t : ℝ => 2 * (n + 1) * aeval t (hermitePhys n))
      volume 0 x := (by fun_prop : Continuous _).intervalIntegrable _ _
  have h := intervalIntegral.integral_eq_sub_of_hasDerivAt (fun t _ => hd t) hi
  rw [intervalIntegral.integral_const_mul] at h
  apply (eq_div_iff (by positivity : (2 * (n + 1) : ℝ) ≠ 0)).mpr
  nlinarith [h]

theorem hermite_weighted_integral (n : ℕ) (x : ℝ) :
    ∫ y : ℝ in (0 : ℝ)..x, Real.exp (-(y ^ 2)) * (aeval y (hermitePhys (n + 1)) : ℝ) =
      aeval (0 : ℝ) (hermitePhys n) - Real.exp (-(x ^ 2)) * aeval x (hermitePhys n) := by
  let g : ℝ → ℝ := fun t => Real.exp (-(t ^ 2)) * aeval t (hermitePhys n)
  have hd (t : ℝ) : HasDerivAt (fun y => -g y)
      (Real.exp (-(t ^ 2)) * aeval t (hermitePhys (n + 1))) t := by
    have hg : DifferentiableAt ℝ g t :=
      (by fun_prop : DifferentiableAt ℝ (fun t : ℝ => Real.exp (-(t ^ 2))) t).mul
        ((hermitePhys n).hasDerivAt_aeval t).differentiableAt
    have ht := hg.hasDerivAt.neg
    change HasDerivAt (fun y => -g y) (-deriv g t) t at ht
    simpa only [g, AAR.OrthogonalPolynomials.hermitePhys_weighted_derivative, neg_mul, neg_neg]
      using ht
  have hi : IntervalIntegrable
      (fun y : ℝ => Real.exp (-(y ^ 2)) * (aeval y (hermitePhys (n + 1)) : ℝ)) volume 0 x :=
    (by fun_prop : Continuous _).intervalIntegrable _ _
  have h := intervalIntegral.integral_eq_sub_of_hasDerivAt (fun t _ => hd t) hi
  simpa only [g, zero_pow (by decide : 2 ≠ 0), neg_zero, Real.exp_zero, one_mul,
    neg_sub_neg] using h

end AARReview
```
