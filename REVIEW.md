# Mathematical review of the AAR project

## Scope and degree of completion

This project develops special functions in Lean, using Andrews, Askey and Roy's
*Special Functions* and the NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions as its
main mathematical sources. Its purpose is to extend Mathlib's coverage, rather
than reproduce either reference sequentially. The principal subjects are Gamma
and Beta functions, Gaussian and confluent hypergeometric functions, Bessel
functions, and orthogonal polynomials. Smaller developments concern Selberg
integrals, spherical harmonics, basic hypergeometric series, partitions, and Bailey
chains. The entry point [AAR.lean](AAR.lean) imports all these areas.

The project has a substantial formally proved core, especially in hypergeometric
continuation, differential and recurrence identities, and general orthogonal
polynomial theory. It also contains a substantial collection of proposed theorem
statements whose proofs are admitted with `sorry`. Some of these statements need
mathematical correction before they can be proved. Thus the project is a mixture
of established formal mathematics and a statement collection under development.

## Mathematical conventions that matter

Several distinctions are essential when reading the code.

**A series and its analytic continuation are different objects.** Mathlib's
ordinary Gaussian hypergeometric function is defined by its power series. An
infinite-sum expression is not, by itself, analytic continuation beyond the
convergence region. The project supplies a separate regularized continuation
`AAR.Hypergeometric.gaussHGCut` on
\(\mathbb C\setminus[1,\infty)\). Identities involving transformed arguments must
either use this continued function or impose convergence conditions on each
series argument. This corresponds to the distinction made in
[DLMF §15.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/15.2).

**Regularization is essential at exceptional parameters.** The ordinary Kummer
function `confluentM` is a totalized series. Its quotient by \(\Gamma(b)\) agrees
with the regularized function away from nonpositive integer \(b\), but its literal
value at a pole need not be the limiting regularized value. Mathlib's
`regularizedHGFun` supplies the appropriate entire coefficient expressions.
Similarly, a product containing a vanishing factor and a Gamma pole cannot be
interpreted as a removable limit merely by multiplying the totalized values.
The normalization follows [DLMF §13.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/13.2).

**Complex powers use a specified branch.** `Complex.slitPlane` excludes the
nonpositive real axis. Powers and logarithms on this domain represent principal
branches; identities obtained by rotations require the corresponding argument
conditions. The logarithmic-argument versions of Tricomi and Whittaker functions
retain information about continuation around the origin that would be lost by
simply replacing an argument by its complex exponential.

**Improper integrals and unordered sums require care.** A Bochner integral is not
a conditionally convergent improper integral. Likewise, an unordered `tsum` is not
automatically the limit of the usual ordered partial sums. The project uses
explicit limits for several oscillatory integrals and boundary series. These
distinctions remain important when adding further integral and asymptotic results.

**Classical polynomial parameters have a restricted interpretation.** The
Laguerre and Jacobi functions are defined by terminating hypergeometric formulas
with Pochhammer prefactors. These formulas give the standard families on the
regular parameter domains, including the usual orthogonality ranges. They do not
automatically provide polynomial continuation in all parameters. For example,
the current definition gives `laguerre (-1) 1 x = 0`, whereas the classically
continued polynomial is \(L_1^{(-1)}(x)=-x\). The degree-zero definitions still give
one. Any extension to exceptional parameters needs a denominator-free finite sum
or a regularized definition, together with comparison theorems.

## Gamma and Beta functions

The [Gamma cluster](AAR/Gamma.lean) builds on Mathlib's Gamma function, Euler and
Beta integrals, reflection formula, duplication formula, and logarithmic
convexity. Its proved contributions include the Weierstrass product for reciprocal
Gamma, Gauss multiplication, digamma recurrence and reflection identities,
several rational and complex special values, polygamma recurrence and reflection,
and the entire Taylor expansion of reciprocal Gamma. The expansion has a
`HasSum` theorem for every complex argument, rather than only a formal coefficient
identity.

The analytic results include Beta integrals on transformed intervals and
half-lines, trigonometric and hyperbolic Beta integrals, Gaussian and Laplace
Gamma integrals, and the asymptotic Gamma ratio
\[
  \frac{\Gamma(t+a)}{\Gamma(t+b)t^{a-b}}\longrightarrow 1
  \qquad(t\to+\infty),
\]
for fixed complex shifts. The Stirling development proves the usual factorial
asymptotic and its extension along fixed translates \(n+t\), with
\(0<t\leq1\). This should not be confused with a complete complex-sector Stirling
theory with arbitrary-order remainder estimates.

The 58 admissions include most of the Binet and further digamma integral
representations, Mellin–Barnes integrals, higher asymptotic expansions, several
Gamma inequalities, multidimensional integrals, and substantial parts of the
Barnes \(G\)-function theory. The reciprocal-Gamma coefficient recurrence and
several zeta expansions are still admitted even though the entire Taylor-series
framework is proved. The \(q\)-Gamma development has product and recurrence
results, but its monotonicity and classical-limit statements remain admitted.

[ContinuedFractions.lean](AAR/Gamma/ContinuedFractions.lean) records seven
tabulated partial numerators for the continued fraction associated with Binet's
function and assigns zero to the remaining coefficients. It therefore represents
a finite truncation; it contains no proof that these convergents approach Binet's
function. A full coefficient sequence is missing mathematical data, rather than
merely a missing convergence proof.

## Gaussian hypergeometric functions

The [hypergeometric cluster](AAR/Hypergeometric.lean) is the most nearly complete
of the large subjects. Its proved theory includes coefficient and terminating-sum
identities, differentiation, the hypergeometric differential equation, contiguous
relations, Euler's integral, Euler and Pfaff transformations, numerous quadratic
transformations and elementary evaluations, and substantial connection-formula
theory, including logarithmic cases.

The principal analytic construction is in
[ArgumentContinuation.lean](AAR/Hypergeometric/ArgumentContinuation.lean).
The regularized function is constructed on the cut plane using Euler integrals
in a suitable parameter region and contiguous relations to shift the parameters.
The code proves analyticity in the argument, agreement with the series on the
unit disk, and uniqueness. Derivative formulas, contiguous relations, Euler's
transformation, and Pfaff's transformation are available on the continued domain.
This is an actual construction of the continued function, not a declaration that
the original series is to be interpreted differently.

Parameter continuation is also developed: the regularized disk function is
entire along entire parameter curves, with separate analyticity and parameter
limits at denominator poles. The single theorem expressing joint complex
differentiability as a map of three complex parameters remains admitted.
The Riemann-equation material includes a hypergeometric solution, gauge shifts,
and Möbius covariance. Boundary-series convergence and limiting behavior near
\(z=1\) have substantial proved treatments with explicit parameter hypotheses.

The 17 admissions consist of joint parameter differentiability, two asymptotic
results, a cubic connection formula, three continued-fraction limits, four special
values, three higher or bilateral summations, and three integral representations.
The summations include Dixon's \({}_3F_2\), Whipple's terminating \({}_7F_6\)
transformation, and Dougall's bilateral formula. These are significant remaining
theorems, despite the small admission count. The classical series formulas and
the cut-plane API also coexist: not every theorem about the series is stated in
the strongest possible continued form.

## Confluent hypergeometric and Whittaker functions

The [confluent cluster](AAR/Confluent.lean) defines Kummer's \(M\), Tricomi's
\(U\), and Whittaker's \(M_{\kappa,\mu}\) and \(W_{\kappa,\mu}\). The proved
Kummer theory includes entire convergence in the argument at regular denominator
parameters, the confluent limit of \({}_2F_1\), Euler's integral, the differential
equation, Kummer's transformation, differentiation, and contiguous relations.
For the regularized function, parameter analyticity permits derivative and
contiguous identities without excluding denominator poles.

Tricomi's function is defined by the connection formula at noninteger second
parameters and by parameter limits at integers. Here the limits are supported by
proofs: [BasicProperties.lean](AAR/Confluent/BasicProperties.lean) proves their
existence at every nonzero argument, including integer second parameters. It also
provides continuity in the parameters, joint continuity on the argument slit
plane, and locally uniform convergence needed for differentiation and parameter
passage. Thus the integer-parameter definition is backed by analysis, rather than
only by a formal limit operator.

Further proved material includes Tricomi transformations, differential and
recurrence identities, terminating cases, several Wronskians, continuation and
jump formulas, and Taylor-based addition formulas. The Whittaker results transfer
much of this structure through their defining transformations. Logarithmic-argument
definitions make the monodromy statements mathematically meaningful.

The full Kummer Laplace transform is proved throughout `Re z > max (0, Re k)`;
[Growth.lean](AAR/Confluent/Growth.lean) supplies the polynomial exponential bound
and holomorphic integral dependence, including denominator poles. The normalized
Tricomi Laplace representation is also proved for `Re a > 0`, `Re z > 0`, and
every complex second parameter. A Gauss confluence limit identifies the integral
with the connection-formula definition, and continuity extends it to integer
parameters. The Whittaker W Laplace representation follows by an affine substitution.

Tricomi's Mellin transform is proved for
`max (0, Re (b - 1)) < Re λ < Re a`, together with absolute integrability.

The 115 admissions are concentrated in integral representations and transforms,
asymptotics, zero distributions, products and additions, and Bessel expansions.
The proved positive-half-plane Laplace representation of \(U\) now supplies a
foundation for positivity, integral estimates, and asymptotic arguments. Many
Mellin and Laplace transforms, Stieltjes representations, and further Whittaker
integrals still require justified exchanges of integrals or continuation.
All four continued-fraction
limit statements remain admitted. The exceptional-parameter defects found in two Bessel-sum statements have been
repaired as described below.

## Bessel functions

The [Bessel cluster](AAR/Bessel.lean) defines \(J_\nu\) and \(I_\nu\) using
regularized \({}_0F_1\) series. It proves their differential equations and
slit-plane analyticity, recurrences and derivative identities for \(J\), its
half-integer evaluations and Poisson integral, and its relation to Kummer's
function. Integer-order reflection and the bilateral generating function are
proved, with a `HasSum` theorem supplying convergence for every complex argument
and every nonzero generating parameter.

[Sonine.lean](AAR/Bessel/Sonine.lean) proves
\[
 \int_0^\infty J_\nu(at)e^{-p^2t^2}t^{\nu+1}\,dt
 =\frac{(a/(2p^2))^\nu}{2p^2}
   e^{-a^2/(4p^2)},
 \qquad \Re\nu>-1,\quad a,p>0.
\]
Its proof supplies reusable Gamma-weighted \({}_0F_1\) integrals and justifies
termwise integration by absolute convergence of a regularized \({}_1F_1\)
majorant series.

The four admissions are Gegenbauer's addition theorem, Nicholson's integral,
infinitely many positive zeros for real order greater than \(-1\), and strict
monotonicity of the first positive zero for nonnegative order. Simplicity of the
positive zeros in the stated range is already proved.

The definitions of \(Y_\nu\) and \(K_\nu\) use parameter limits at integer
orders. Unlike the corresponding Tricomi construction, existence of these limits
is not yet proved. Their differential-equation theorems consequently cover
noninteger orders. This is a foundational gap particularly relevant to Nicholson's
integral, which uses \(K_0\). Mathlib itself also defines `Complex.besselJ`; the
project's definition uses the same normalization but is a separate declaration.

## Orthogonal polynomials

The [orthogonal-polynomial cluster](AAR/OrthogonalPolynomials.lean) has both an
abstract measure-theoretic theory and a large collection of named families.
`IsOrthogonalPolynomials` requires exact degree, integrability of squares,
strictly positive squared norms, and pairwise orthogonality. These hypotheses
exclude degenerate measures and provide a polynomial basis.

The abstract results include the three-term recurrence, real and simple roots,
positive-weight Gauss quadrature exact below degree \(2n\), and the
Christoffel–Darboux identity. Additional proved results concern reproducing
kernels, positive Hankel determinants, recurrence coefficients and squared norms,
and parity for symmetric measures. Parseval and convergence of the finite
expansions are proved for compactly supported measures, as is orthogonality of
the kernel polynomials with the modified weight. Favard's converse theorem,
interlacing, and several determinant formulas remain admitted. Completeness
is not claimed for an arbitrary orthogonality measure.

For the classical families, the physicists' Hermite polynomials are genuine
polynomials over the integers and are related to Mathlib's probabilists' Hermite
polynomials. The code proves Rodrigues and generating-function identities,
orthogonality and norms, derivative and recurrence formulas, and several
addition, multiplication, linearization, and integral identities. Laguerre and
Jacobi orthogonality are proved in their classical real parameter ranges.
Laguerre generating functions and squared norms are proved, as are the weighted
Laguerre convolution, Jacobi squared norms, both Rodrigues formulas, and Gegenbauer
orthogonality, including its degree-zero norm. The differential
equations and a substantial set of raising, lowering, reflection, and derivative
formulas for the classical families are proved. There are explicit bridges to
Mathlib's Chebyshev and shifted Legendre polynomials. Normalized Gegenbauer and
Legendre Turán inequalities are also proved.

The wider family coverage includes Hahn, Krawtchouk, Meixner, Charlier, continuous
Hahn, Meixner–Pollaczek, Wilson and continuous dual Hahn polynomials, several
\(q\)-families, associated and exceptional families, disk and triangle
polynomials, and orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle. Much of the finite
algebra is available, but many orthogonality measures, norms, and generating
functions remain to be established. On the unit circle, Szegő recurrences,
the bound on Verblunsky coefficients, and norm ratios are proved; the converse
existence theorem is admitted.

There are 102 admissions. The largest concentrations are bounds and zeros (23),
integral and transform identities (22), and series identities (11). All four
Mehler–Heine limit statements are admitted. General root theory
does not automatically discharge the separate statements locating the zeros of
each named family in its natural interval. Establishing systematic connections
between the abstract polynomial API and the function-valued classical definitions
would make this proved general theory more broadly usable.

## Other subjects

**Basic hypergeometric series.** The [\(q\)-series development](AAR/QSeries.lean)
contains finite and infinite \(q\)-Pochhammer symbols, convergence and
nonvanishing results for the products, Jackson integration, and definitions of
basic hypergeometric series. The \(q\)-binomial theorem, Heine's transformation,
and the \(q\)-Beta integral are proved. Jacobi's triple product, its theta-function
form, and Ramanujan's \({}_1\psi_1\) summation are the three admissions. Infinite
products and bilateral series are therefore represented, but their principal
bilateral identities remain open.

**Selberg integrals.** The [Selberg files](AAR/Selberg.lean) define the integral
and record its evaluation, Aomoto's extension, discriminant and inequality
applications, a terminating summation, and finite-field identities. The
one-variable Beta specialization is proved. The general Selberg and Aomoto
evaluations remain admitted, as do the five named applications. Related
Dirichlet-simplex, Laguerre, Hermite, and circular multiple integrals are located
in the Gamma cluster and are also admitted. The presence of product formulas
should not be read as a completed multidimensional integration theory.

**Spherical harmonics.** The [spherical-harmonic files](AAR/SphericalHarmonics.lean)
define the polynomial Laplacian, homogeneous harmonic polynomials, and the
surface-measure inner product on the Euclidean sphere. The surface-area
normalization in three dimensions is proved. The function
`harmonicHomogeneousDim` is a combinatorial formula, with its value \(2k+1\)
proved in three variables; it is not yet identified with the vector-space
dimension of harmonic homogeneous polynomials. Orthogonality of different
degrees, the addition theorem, Funk–Hecke, the Gegenbauer addition formula,
Poisson reproduction, and the double covering \(SU(2)\to SO(3)\) remain admitted.

**Partitions and Bailey chains.** [Partitions.lean](AAR/Partitions.lean) uses
Mathlib's partition type and imports its generating-function, Glaisher, and
pentagonal-number theory. It defines the partition-counting function and proves
its value at zero. The three Ramanujan congruences are admitted.
[Bailey.lean](AAR/Bailey.lean) defines Bailey pairs; both Rogers–Ramanujan
identities, Bailey's lemma, and Watson's terminating transformation are admitted.

## Statement defects repaired on 2026-10-07

The review identified the following concrete counterexamples. Their statements
have now been repaired; all three corrected identities still require proofs.

1. **The Gamma sine integral at zero.** In
   [Gamma/IntegralRepresentations.lean](AAR/Gamma/IntegralRepresentations.lean),
   `AAR.Gamma.Gamma_sin` formerly permitted \(-1<x<1\), including \(x=0\), and gave the
   limit as `Real.Gamma x * Real.sin (π * x / 2)`. At zero that expression is zero
   in Lean, whereas the improper integral is
   \(\int_0^\infty\sin(t)/t\,dt=\pi/2\).
   The classical formula [DLMF 5.9.7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.9.E7) requires a
   removable-singularity interpretation there; compare the sine-integral
   definitions and limit in [DLMF §6.2(ii)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/6.2.ii).
   The repaired statement uses the regular expression
   \(\pi/(2\Gamma(1-x)\cos(\pi x/2))\) throughout the original interval,
   giving \(\pi/2\) at zero.

2. **Two finite Kummer–Bessel sums at a Gamma pole.** In
   [Confluent/Relations.lean](AAR/Confluent/Relations.lean), both
   `AAR.Confluent.confluentM_besselI_sum` and
   `AAR.Confluent.confluentM_besselI_sum_neg` formerly allowed \(\nu=0,n=0,z=1\).
   Their denominator-parameter hypotheses were satisfied. Each right-hand side
   is zero because it contains the totalized factor \(\Gamma(0)\), while the
   left-hand side is \(M(1/2,1,2)>0\): its convergent defining series has constant
   term one and positive remaining terms. The repaired statements exclude every
   nonpositive integer \(\nu\), or \(\nu-n\), respectively, through the added
   `hΓ` hypothesis. Their denominator and argument hypotheses are preserved.

These examples are not an exhaustive certification or rejection of the other
admissions. Further checking is particularly important for endpoint cases,
Gamma-quotient cancellations, branch-dependent transformations, asymptotic
remainder scales, and empty or singleton index sets in multivariate formulas.
DLMF equation attributes document a source association; they do not certify the
translation of its conventions into Lean.

## Full half-plane Kummer transform (2026-10-08)

`laplace_regularizedHGFun` is now proved with its original hypotheses
`Re b > 0`, `Re z > Re k`, and `Re z > 0`. Its region includes transformed
arguments outside the unit disk, and arbitrary denominator parameters remain
covered. No statement or assumption was changed.

The new [Growth.lean](AAR/Confluent/Growth.lean) starts with Euler's integral and
the all-parameter reciprocal-Gamma recurrence. Denominator lowering, followed by
integration of the Kummer derivative, proves a bound
`C * (1+t)^N * exp (max (0, Re k) * t)` for every positive ray and every complex
pair of parameters. Finite Gamma majorants then prove absolute convergence and
holomorphic dependence on `z` throughout the full half-plane. Analytic uniqueness
extends the earlier series-region transform, and an explicit argument calculation
keeps `k/z` on the Gauss cut plane.

For the remaining `confluentU_laplace` priority, the normalized integral is now
proved continuous in the second parameter. A fixed polynomial majorant controls
a neighborhood of every parameter. The proved lemma
`confluentU_laplace_of_eqOn_integerComplement` therefore reduces the general
representation to its connection-formula identification at noninteger second
parameters. That identification remains open; the reduction is a conditional
supporting result, not a proof of the admitted general representation.

## Mathematical priorities

The most useful next steps are those that connect the existing proved theories
and discharge common analytic obligations.

- **Continue reviewing exceptional parameters.**
  Regularized Gamma and hypergeometric expressions should replace literal
  pole–zero products when the intended assertion includes a removable limit.
  Classical polynomial continuation likewise needs a deliberate definition.
- **Use Tricomi's Laplace representation.** Its Mellin transform is now proved;
  further Laplace transforms, positivity, and large-argument estimates remain.
  These require integrable majorants for exchanging integrals or passing to
  limits. The corresponding Whittaker \(W\) formulas can then reuse the defining
  transformation and the proved Laplace representation.
- **Connect classical polynomial families to the abstract orthogonality API.**
  The now-proved Jacobi norms and Rodrigues formulas, polynomial-valued
  realizations, and completeness on compact intervals would make the quadrature and root
  theory easier to apply. Several finite-sum and generating-function admissions
  are also accessible through the established coefficient methods.
- **Establish Gamma remainder and Mellin–Barnes tools.** Bounds for Gamma ratios,
  vertical-line decay, and justified contour or improper-integral operations
  would serve several subjects at once. The higher asymptotic assertions need
  quantified remainders, not just leading-order heuristics.
- **Complete the integer-order foundations of Bessel \(Y\) and \(K\).** Parameter
  analyticity and removable singularities would make the current limit
  definitions usable at integer orders. Integral representations and
  oscillation/comparison theory would then support Nicholson's formula and the
  zero theorems.
- **Treat the smaller subjects as substantive developments.** Favard and
  Verblunsky existence, general Selberg evaluation, spherical-harmonic dimension
  and addition theory, and the bilateral \(q\)-identities each require a coherent
  mathematical argument. Their small numbers of admissions do not indicate small
  amounts of missing theory.

For navigation, the subject import files and linked implementation files describe
the available objects, while the statements and their hypotheses determine the
precise scope of each theorem. The strongest reusable assets are the regularized
hypergeometric continuation machinery, the coefficient and analytic-uniqueness
methods for differential equations, the Gamma-weighted integration techniques,
and the measure-theoretic theory of orthogonal polynomials.
